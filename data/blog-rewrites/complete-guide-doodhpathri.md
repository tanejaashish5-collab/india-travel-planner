# Complete guide to Doodhpathri

Short answer: Doodhpathri is a **day trip from Srinagar** (42 km, 2,730 m) and there is **no permit or visitor quota**, despite what some guides say. Go in **May, June or September (10/10 on our ledger)**, on a weekday, and check that it is open before you leave: in 2026 it shut for over a month from late July.

## What it is

A flat alpine meadow in Budgam district, named for the milk-white colour of the water running through it. The J&K government's own district site gives the distance as 42 km from Srinagar and the route as Srinagar to Budgam, Budgam to Khansahib, then Khansahib to Doodhpathri via Raiyar.

Our data rates it easy, with almost no infrastructure. Plan it as half a day to one day out of Srinagar, not a place to stay.

## Is there a permit? No

Older versions of this guide, and several others online, say entry is limited by a daily permit quota. We could not find one. The Budgam district page lists no permit, fee or cap, and the 2026 reopening report we read mentions none.

What does exist is a vehicle toll at the gate. The amount is not published anywhere official, so ask at the gate. Visitor reports describe parking and entry charges and union charges for ponies. Treat any specific rupee figure online as unconfirmed.

## When to go

Our month-by-month ledger, out of 10:

- **May, June, September: 10.** May is peak wildflower, June is green with snow peaks behind, September is the clearest air of the year and the best month for photography.
- **April and October: 8.** April has the meadow emerging from snow with the stream running full. October is golden, 5 to 15°C, and the last good month.
- **July and August: 6.** Light monsoon showers, a muddy road from Budgam, meadow still green. Our full take is in [Doodhpathri in August](/en/destination/doodhpathri/august).
- **March: 4, November: 2.** Snow or first snow, roads unreliable, facilities shutting.
- **December to February: closed.** Snowbound, no road access.

Weekdays matter more than the month. Locals crowd the meadow on summer weekends, and Monday to Thursday is our best-day call.

## Check it is open first

Doodhpathri can close without warning. Rising Kashmir reported it reopened on 22 August 2026 after more than a month shut, with tourism activity affected since the end of July and no reason given. Ask your hotel or driver in Srinagar the day before, and have a Gulmarg or Srinagar plan B.

## Getting there

- **By car:** 42 km from Srinagar. Our data says about 1.5 hours; the district site says under 2 to 3 hours by car or bus, so plan for the longer end. The road is good to Khansahib, then a fair mountain road. A hatchback is fine, but the last stretch is narrow and can be rough.
- **Fuel:** the nearest petrol pump is Khansahib, 22 km short of the meadow. Fill up before.
- **Last stretch:** reports differ on how far your own vehicle can go. Expect to be stopped at a parking and toll point and to cover the remainder on foot, by pony or by ATV.
- **Without a car:** shared cabs run from Khansahib. We found no direct bus confirmed. A hired taxi from Srinagar for the day is the practical option.

## On the ground

- **Stay:** there are government-run huts at Doodhpathri, bookable through JKTDC (jktdc.co.in). The district website lists the CEO Doodhpathri on 01951-256294. Our own data lists no formal accommodation, so confirm by phone before you count on a bed. Most visitors stay in Srinagar.
- **Food:** bring a packed lunch and water. Seasonal stalls and tea sellers come and go. Kashmiri wazwan is a Srinagar meal.
- **Ponies:** our ledger has ₹300 to 800 for a ride. Visitor reports run higher, roughly ₹600 to 1,500 after bargaining, with ATVs above that. Agree the price before you get on.
- **Network:** weak. Jio and BSNL work patchily, Airtel and Vi mostly do not. Download offline maps, and tell someone your plan.
- **Medical:** our data lists District Hospital Budgam, 35 km away. A primary health centre at Raiyar is reported closer. Carry a basic first-aid kit. Emergency number: 112 (ambulance 108).
- **ATMs:** none.

## With kids

Our family verdict is **4 out of 5, from about age 3**. The ground is flat, the streams are shallow and the place is safe. The constraints are practical: zero facilities, a rough final stretch, and a day that can be long for small children. Go early, pack food, water and layers (it is cold in the evening even in summer, lows around 8°C), and keep the day short. A stroller will not work on this ground.

## What it costs

On our cost ledger a budget day runs about **₹1,150** and a midrange day about **₹3,700**, mostly the taxi from Srinagar, food and a pony ride. Real cost depends on the taxi price you agree and whether you ride.

## The one-line answer

Go on a weekday in May, June or September, confirm it is open, bring your own food, and treat it as a half-day from Srinagar.
