# Is the Girnar climb worth it?

Worth doing — but only in the right months, and the step count most people quote is slightly off. NakshIQ's dataset logs the Girnar ascent consistently as a **9,999-step climb**, not the rounder 10,000 you'll see on most travel sites. We don't have a verified kilometre figure for the route in our data, so we're not going to guess one — the honest answer is the step count, not a converted distance.

## The step count, and why it matters

The climb runs from the base at Girnar Taleti up basalt steps to Datta peak at the summit, elevation 1,117m. Across NakshIQ's month-by-month notes for [Girnar](/en/destination/girnar/december), the figure that shows up consistently in the detailed weather-and-access text is 9,999 steps — a number with its own religious significance in Jain and Hindu tradition, which is likely why it persists as "9,999" rather than getting rounded.

## When it's a go: November–February, plus January

Four months score a clean 5/5 (10/10 displayed). January and February are the sharpest windows — 10–29°C, dry basalt, clear summit views, and the ropeway running its full daily schedule for anyone who wants to skip a leg of the climb. November adds the Girnar Parikrama, a five-day, 36km circumambulation that draws roughly two million Hindu and Jain pilgrims — by far the region's largest religious gathering, and worth building a trip around deliberately rather than stumbling into. December closes the peak window at 12–26°C with what the dataset calls the year's sharpest light on Datta peak.

## When it's a skip: April–September

Six straight months score 1–2/5. April and May turn the exposed basalt steps genuinely dangerous — surface temperatures climb past 50°C by mid-morning, and the dataset flags real heat-stroke risk on the unshaded sections. June through August add the monsoon: wet rock kills grip on the steps, and July specifically notes the climb is officially closed. September is still recovering — the steps stay wet and slippery even as the rain itself tapers off.

## The shoulder months

March holds at "wait" (3/5) — the climb is still doable but needs a start before 4am to beat the heat on the exposed sections. October mirrors it from the other direction: the post-monsoon reopening, but steps can still be damp in patches depending on how recently the rain cleared.

## What it costs

Peak season (November–February): a mid-range hotel runs a typical ₹6,000 a night (range ₹3,500–10,500). Budget travelers have real options nearby — Shri Bharati Ashram at Girnar Taleti and Hotel Asopalav in Bhavnath are both real, listed stays at the base. Off-peak rates (April–September) drop by roughly half. Full breakdown on the [cost page](/en/cost/girnar).

## The one-line answer

Climb between November and February, start before dawn regardless of month, and if anyone tells you it's exactly 10,000 steps — it's 9,999, one short of the round number, and the dataset is consistent about it.
