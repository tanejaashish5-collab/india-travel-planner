# Is Zuluk in December worth it?

Short answer: not really, unless you're set on the first week. Our dataset scores [Zuluk in December](/en/destination/zuluk/december) at **4/10 — skip**, and the reasoning is specific: very cold (-5 to 8°C), snow arriving, and the road may close by late December.

## What the month actually looks like

Early December is still workable — our own why-go note calls it "still accessible, snow scenery" — but the why-not is just as direct: upper-altitude passes on the Old Silk Route can close on snow days, and the recommendation is to verify road status 24 hours before you go. The dataset's own best-months window for Zuluk is March through May and October through December, so December technically sits inside that window on paper, even though its specific score (4/10) is the lowest month in that six-month stretch.

## The context that matters

December isn't Zuluk's story at its best. April is the dataset's top month at **10/10** — "road clear, Kanchenjunga views, rhododendrons" — with March and May close behind at 8/10. October and November both hold at a clean **8/10**, "post-monsoon," "Kanchenjunga crystal clear." December then drops to 4/10 as snow starts arriving, and January and February bottom out at **2/10** — "extreme cold, road blocked by snow, not accessible." June through September are also 2/10, for the opposite reason: monsoon landslides damage the road rather than snow blocking it.

So December reads as a transition month between Zuluk's two good windows, not a destination-ending skip — but it's genuinely the weakest of the six "open" months rather than a comfortable extension of November.

## The wider road picture, mid-November 2026

We don't have a road update logged for the Zuluk/Old Silk Route segment specifically, but the East Sikkim high-altitude network it sits within saw real disruption going into winter this year. On November 12, heavy snowfall stranded more than 900 tourists at Nathu La and Tsomgo Lake — the next high pass over — and the Indian Army completed the rescue by November 13. Separately, NH-10, the Siliguri-Gangtok lifeline that feeds the whole region, has been under a nightly 9pm-6am closure since November 13 for slope-protection work, running through December 13.

Treat this as regional context rather than a direct reading of the Zuluk road itself — we don't have a dated update for that specific stretch. But it's a concrete, dated illustration of exactly the risk our December note is flagging: once snow starts in these passes, conditions can turn fast.

## What it costs

Zuluk's peak-cost months are November through December and March through May — a mid-range room runs a typical **₹4,800/night** (range ₹2,750-8,150), a homestay around ₹2,800, and a hostel dorm bed at roughly ₹500. Food runs about ₹550 a day, a full-day taxi is ₹3,650, and permits for this border-adjacent route add a flat ₹300. Full breakdown on the [Zuluk cost page](/en/cost/zuluk).

## Getting there

Zuluk's nearest airport is Bagdogra (170km) or Pakyong (75km); the nearest railhead is New Jalpaiguri (185km). Most trips run 2-3 days threading the Old Silk Route's switchbacks, and permits are required for this stretch of East Sikkim near the border.

## Verdict

- **Early December:** workable if your window is short and you check the road status first — our own note calls early-month access fine, snow scenery included.
- **Late December:** skip — the closure risk the dataset flags is real, and a neighbouring pass already proved this season how fast 900+ people can get stranded.
- **Flexible dates:** October and November both score a clean 8/10 with clear Kanchenjunga views and none of the late-month closure risk; April is the dataset's single best month at 10/10.
