# Khaliya Top trek: distance, height and how long it takes

Short answer: **10km** round trip, a maximum altitude of **3,500m**, done over **2 days**, rated **moderate** difficulty. Khaliya Top sits above Munsiyari in Uttarakhand's Kumaon Himalaya, and the numbers surprise people who expect a short ridge walk rather than a genuine two-day climb with real altitude to manage.

## The numbers

Our trek dataset logs Khaliya Top at 10km for the round trip, a 2-day duration, and a peak altitude of 3,500m — moderate difficulty, and not recommended for children under 10. That altitude is the thing people underestimate: 3,500m is high enough that acclimatisation and pacing matter, even though the distance itself (10km return) is short by Himalayan trek standards. The route takes in Panchachuli at eye level, alpine meadow, and seasonal wildflowers, which is the payoff for the climb.

## When to go

Our best-months data for Khaliya Top is April-May and September-October — a narrower window than many lower-altitude treks, because the 3,500m summit still carries real snow and cold risk outside those months. Munsiyari itself, the base town, shares the same best-months pattern (April-May, September-October) and sits at 2,200m, with July and August flagged to avoid across the region for monsoon conditions.

## What it costs and takes to arrange

No permit is required — Munsiyari is not in a restricted area. Budget trekkers going self-guided from Munsiyari, carrying their own food, are looking at roughly ₹1,000. Hiring a local guide (around ₹800/day from Munsiyari) brings the cost closer to ₹3,000. A full operator package — transport from Kathgodam, stay, meals and guide included — runs closer to ₹6,000. Getting to the start point means Delhi to Kathgodam by train (₹400-700, about 7 hours), then Kathgodam to Munsiyari by shared taxi (₹700, roughly 10 hours via Almora and Birthi Falls) — or a direct Delhi-Munsiyari bus (₹1,000, about 16 hours) if you'd rather skip the change.

## Practical notes before you go

Network coverage is the one honest gap: BSNL works at Munsiyari town, Jio is patchy, and there's no signal at all on the trail or at Khaliya Top itself — Airtel doesn't reach the area. The nearest medical point is the PHC in Munsiyari itself (0km), with the district hospital in Pithoragarh, about 90km and a 4-hour drive, for anything serious. Plan the trek with that in mind rather than assuming a call for help is an option mid-route.

## The one-line answer

10km, 3,500m, 2 days, moderate — go April-May or September-October, carry what you need for a signal-free trail, and budget from ₹1,000 self-guided to ₹6,000 for a full operator package.
