# Is the Ahobilam trek worth it?

Yes, in the right months. The climb from Lower to Upper Ahobilam is **7km one-way** through Nallamala forest to the Lakshmi Narasimha shrine — the anchor leg of the wider Nava Narasimha circuit, nine temples scattered across the gorge. NakshIQ's dataset scores it a clean go (5/5, or 10/10 on the site's displayed scale) for five months of the year, and a hard skip for three.

## The nine-temple circuit, and where the trek fits

Ahobilam isn't one temple — it's a scattered complex split into Lower Ahobilam (temple-and-ritual, accessible year-round) and Upper Ahobilam (the Lakshmi Narasimha shrine, reached only by the 7km forest trek). The full Nava Narasimha circuit links all nine shrines across both levels. When the [destination page](/en/destination/ahobilam/december) marks a month "skip," it's specifically the Upper trek and the full circuit that collapse — Lower Ahobilam darshan keeps running regardless.

## When it's a go: October–February

Five months score 5/5. October opens the window as the northeast monsoon arrives as evening showers only, with the trek at what the dataset calls year-best grip on dry trail. November adds Karthika Masam — a full month of Telugu Vaishnavite pilgrim density at the Nava Narasimha cluster. December through February hold the same clean conditions, with December logged as the coolest and most comfortable stretch (16–26°C).

## When it's a skip: May–June

Two months score 1/5. May is Nallamala's heat peak — 26–40°C, with the dataset noting the gorge trail becomes impossible and forest-fire risk climbs on the range. June brings the southwest monsoon in hard: 150–220mm of rain, a leech-heavy trail, and landslide risk on the exposed sections. Both months keep Lower Ahobilam open; neither supports the trek.

## The shoulder stretch

March still works (4/5) but compresses to a pre-9am climb as daytime heat pushes to 32–34°C. April drops to "wait" as the trek becomes impossible past 7am. July and August sit at "wait" too — the monsoon is technically still running, but rainfall eases enough that Lower Ahobilam and ritual-focused visits hold up even if the Upper trek stays risky. September is the bridge month: monsoon retreating, but the trail needs roughly two to three dry weeks to firm back up before the trek reopens properly in October.

## What it costs

In peak season (October–March), a mid-range hotel runs a typical ₹5,700 a night (range ₹3,300–9,800), a homestay around ₹3,300, and food about ₹1,200 a day. Off-peak (May–August) rates roughly halve. Full breakdown on the [cost page](/en/cost/ahobilam).

## The one-line answer

If the 7km trek and the full nine-temple circuit are the point of the trip, keep it to October through February. Outside that window, Lower Ahobilam alone is still a real visit — just not the same one.
