# Is Kolkata in November worth it?

Short answer: our ledger scores [Kolkata in November](/en/destination/kolkata/november) at a clean **10/10 — go**. The city's biggest festival, Durga Puja, has just wrapped, the humidity has broken, and the weather settles into 22–28°C — about as comfortable as Kolkata gets.

## What November actually looks like

Kolkata sits at near sea level (9m), so its calendar is really a heat-and-humidity calendar more than anything else. Our ledger lists October through March as the city's workable window, and November sits inside the best stretch of it: cool, dry, and — per the note on file — the city's "most walkable and relaxed" month, with the post-Puja crowds having thinned out.

There's no caveat attached to this month on our ledger — no "why not" flagged at all, which is unusual for a city this size. It's listed as suited for families at 10/10 as well, for travellers weighing a multi-generation trip.

November is also when two onward routes open up properly: the Darjeeling–Kalimpong–Gangtok hill circuit is fully accessible by this point in the season, and the Sundarbans' cooler-weather window (more comfortable boat days, more active wildlife) begins. If Kolkata is a stopover before either of those, November is the month that makes both work.

## What it costs

The [Kolkata cost page](/en/cost/kolkata) has the full season-by-season breakdown; for a quick reference, budget travellers are looking at roughly ₹500 food + ₹310 stay + ₹280 transport + ₹200 activities per person per day (≈₹1,290 total). Mid-range runs closer to ₹1,200 food + ₹1,150 stay + ₹900 transport + ₹730 activities (≈₹3,980/day), and a splurge trip — proper hotels, a hired car, better food — lands near ₹10,700/day. None of that is a November premium specifically; it's the general shoulder-to-peak range, and November sits on the higher-demand side of it since it's prime tourist season.

A trip here suits 3–5 days comfortably, per our ideal-duration note — enough time for North Kolkata's old lanes, the Victoria Memorial and Maidan, and a proper sweets-and-street-food circuit without rushing it.

## Who this suits

Kolkata in November works for food-first travellers (this is built around Bengali cuisine and sweets, not spectacle), for anyone staging a Northeast or Darjeeling-hills trip, and for families — the comfortable weather does a lot of the work that other months can't. It doesn't particularly suit anyone chasing a single headline sight; Kolkata's appeal here is cumulative — old architecture, food, and the Hooghly riverfront, rather than one must-see.

## Verdict

- **Want comfortable weather and a city that rewards walking:** go now — this is close to the top of Kolkata's calendar.
- **Staging a Darjeeling/Sikkim or Sundarbans trip:** November is the month both legs open up together.
- **Chasing a single spectacle (Durga Puja itself):** that's October, not November — you'll get the calm aftermath here, not the festival.
