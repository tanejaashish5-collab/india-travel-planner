# Is Darjeeling in August worth it?

Short answer: our ledger scores [Darjeeling in August](/en/destination/darjeeling/august) at **2/10 — skip**. This is peak monsoon in the hills, and it shows in almost everything that makes Darjeeling worth the trip.

## What August actually looks like

The monsoon here isn't a passing shower pattern — it's relentless. On our ledger:

- **Outdoor activities are effectively cancelled.** Viewpoints, ridge walks, and the toy train's scenic runs all depend on visibility and dry track, neither of which August reliably delivers.
- **Roads turn treacherous.** Hill roads in and around Darjeeling are landslide-prone through the monsoon.
- **Tea gardens close for their monsoon break.** If a tea-estate visit was the point of the trip, August removes it.

The one thing in August's favour is price — accommodation runs at its lowest rates of the year, since this is the off-season by every other measure.

## Who this still works for

Realistically: almost no one whose plans depend on being outdoors. If you're passing through for a specific indoor reason — a stay at altitude to escape plains heat, work you can do from a hill-town cafe — August is cheap and quiet. Everyone else should expect a trip built around waiting out weather rather than seeing anything.

Getting here: nearest airport is Bagdogra (67km), nearest railhead is New Jalpaiguri (74km). Plan 3–4 days if you're committing to the season regardless — our ledger puts a budget day near ₹1,250, mid-range near ₹4,000, and a full heritage-tea-town trip near ₹10,800; see the [Darjeeling cost page](/en/cost/darjeeling) for the breakdown.

## The October and November case

This is the part worth reading before booking. [Darjeeling in October](/en/destination/darjeeling/october) scores a full **10/10 — go**: 10–18°C, crystal-clear post-monsoon air, Kanchenjunga visible from Tiger Hill at sunrise, the toy train running, and second-flush tea in season. [November](/en/destination/darjeeling/november) holds at the same **10/10**, with clear skies continuing, autumn-flush tea, and the Singalila Ridge trek at its best.

Both months sit inside Darjeeling's own best-months window on our data (March–May and October–November) — August and the surrounding monsoon months are the ones the ledger flags to avoid.

## Verdict

- **Fixed August dates:** skip unless your trip is entirely indoors — the outdoor product Darjeeling sells isn't available.
- **Flexible dates:** wait for October or November. Both score 10/10 against August's 2/10 — this isn't a close call.
