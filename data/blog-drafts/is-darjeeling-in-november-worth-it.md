# Is Darjeeling in November worth it?

Short answer: yes. Our ledger scores [Darjeeling in November](/en/destination/darjeeling/november) at **10/10 — go**. It's the calmer half of Darjeeling's autumn peak, and the case for it is straightforward.

## What November actually looks like

Temperatures sit at 5-15°C, with frost appearing by late month. Kanchenjunga visibility stays excellent — some regulars say it's sharper than October, since winter air has settled in by now. The autumn tea harvest wraps up over the month as estates begin their winter shutdown, so this is close to the last window for tea-garden visits before spring flush. Tourist numbers thin out noticeably once the Diwali-Dussehra rush passes, and the toy train runs its scheduled service with half-empty carriages rather than the queues October sees.

Darjeeling also has real autumn foliage on its deciduous trees, which surprises most first-time Indian visitors — this isn't a purely evergreen hill station.

## What's worth doing

On our ledger, November's standout activities are the Singalila Ridge trek for unobstructed Kanchenjunga views, a Tiger Hill sunrise (colder than October's, but the sky is often clearer), tea-garden walks during the last of the autumn flush, and the toy train run to Ghum in clean autumn light. Hotel rates start their winter descent through the month — worth negotiating for stays of three nights or more, since operators would rather fill rooms at a discount than leave them empty.

## Who should hold off

By month-end the cold turns serious — below 5°C at night — which rules out families with very young children and anyone who finds sub-8°C evenings genuinely uncomfortable. Tea tourism enthusiasts specifically should note the gardens are shutting down for winter, not opening: if a tea-estate visit is the whole point of the trip, earlier in the season serves that better.

## The context that matters

Darjeeling's own data shows this isn't a fluke month. The two months either side of the monsoon — August and September — score **2/10** and **4/10** respectively on our ledger: persistent rain limits Kanchenjunga visibility to almost nothing, tea gardens close for the season, and hill trails turn leech-heavy and unreliable. November sits inside Darjeeling's broader best-months window (March–May and October–November) rather than being an isolated good week, which is part of why it holds up as a genuine recommendation rather than a narrow exception.

Getting here: nearest airport is Bagdogra (67km), nearest railhead is New Jalpaiguri (74km). Plan 3–4 days. Our ledger puts a budget day near ₹1,250, mid-range near ₹4,000, and a fuller heritage-tea-town trip near ₹10,800 — see the [Darjeeling cost page](/en/cost/darjeeling) for the full breakdown.

## Verdict

- **Fixed November dates:** go — this is one of Darjeeling's two strongest months on record.
- **Choosing between October and November:** both score well; November trades a slightly bigger crowd for a colder, clearer sky and the tail end of tea season.
- **Anything June–September:** wait. The monsoon genuinely closes down what makes the trip worth it.
