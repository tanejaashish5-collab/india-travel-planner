# Is Nelliyampathy in August worth it?

Short answer: our ledger scores [Nelliyampathy in August](/en/destination/nelliyampathy/august) at **2/10 — skip**. This small Kerala hill station holds onto its July monsoon pattern through August, with a festival closure layered on top.

## What August actually looks like

Rainfall runs 700–900mm for the month, and the effects compound: the 12-hairpin-bend approach road is landslide-prone, plantation walks suspend, and visibility from the viewpoints that make Nelliyampathy worth the drive is unreliable. Temperatures stay mild, 20–26°C, but that's cold comfort when the roads and trails aren't cooperating.

Onam — Kerala's harvest festival, which falls in August or September depending on the year — adds a second complication on our ledger: it shuts most local operations for three to four days, on top of the weather.

## Why this destination is different from most monsoon skips

Nelliyampathy has essentially no resort infrastructure — that's part of its appeal in season, when the silence and empty estates are the draw. But it means there's no indoor-first fallback the way a bigger hill station might offer. Off-season here isn't "quieter but workable" — the destination's whole product depends on functioning roads and open trails, and August takes both away.

Getting here: nearest airport is Coimbatore (100km), nearest railhead is Palakkad Junction (55km). Our ledger estimates a budget day near ₹1,000 and a mid-range day near ₹2,500 — see the [Nelliyampathy cost page](/en/cost/nelliyampathy). Plan 2–3 days for a proper visit, but not in August.

## When to actually go

The ledger is consistent here: September is a "wait" month at 3/10 as the monsoon withdraws and the road stabilises mid-month, but [October](/en/destination/nelliyampathy/october) jumps to **8/10 — go** as the season opens fully, plantation walks turn their greenest, and the road settles. [November](/en/destination/nelliyampathy/november) and [December](/en/destination/nelliyampathy/december) both score **10/10**, dry and clear, though hotel rates climb toward Christmas and New Year.

If you can only travel in the monsoon months, the honest answer is to pick a different Kerala hill station with better road infrastructure — Nelliyampathy's single access road is the whole bottleneck.

## Verdict

- **Fixed August dates:** skip — the road and trails are the product, and both are compromised.
- **Flexible dates:** October through March is the real season, peaking November–December.
