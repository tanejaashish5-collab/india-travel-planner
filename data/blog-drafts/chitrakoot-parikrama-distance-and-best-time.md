# The Chitrakoot parikrama — distance, route, and when to walk it

Short answer: the Kamadgiri parikrama at [Chitrakoot](/en/destination/chitrakoot) is a **5km circuit**, walked around the base of Kamadgiri hill, traditionally on foot and barefoot. Most pilgrims complete it in 1.5 to 4 hours depending on pace and how many stops they make at the shrines along the way.

## The route

The circuit loops the base of Kamadgiri — the hill Hindu tradition holds as Kamadgiri, the wish-fulfilling form of the mountain associated with Rama's exile — and is shared across the Uttar Pradesh/Madhya Pradesh border. Along the way it passes four major temple complexes: Prachin Mukharvind, Bharat Milap, Dwitiya Mukharvind, and Choupada, plus Baraha ke Hanuman Ji and smaller shrines including Lakshman Pahari, Sakshi Gopal, and Charan Paduka. The path itself is paved stone, but uneven in places — worth knowing given the barefoot tradition.

Two access notes worth having before you go: e-rickshaws run the outer ring-road sections, but the inner circuit is foot-only, and palanquin (palki) help is reported for elderly or mobility-limited pilgrims as a local, informal arrangement rather than a fixed service. No permit is required to cross the state border partway through the route.

## When to actually walk it

This is where the ledger diverges from a plain distance answer. Chitrakoot sits at just 317m elevation in the Bundelkhand region, and the climate swings hard across the year.

[May](/en/destination/chitrakoot/may) and [June](/en/destination/chitrakoot/june) score **2/10 — skip** on our ledger: 28–47°C, the exposed trail genuinely dangerous in afternoon heat, and the Mandakini river running low. Barefoot walking in that heat is a real dehydration risk, not a figure of speech. Monsoon months (July–September) bring their own problem — flooding and a swollen Mandakini that makes the ghats risky, separate from the heat issue.

[October](/en/destination/chitrakoot/october) scores a clean **10/10 — go**: 20–32°C, post-monsoon river levels, and the ledger calls the parikrama itself "comfortable" in that window. [November](/en/destination/chitrakoot/november) also scores **10/10 — go**, cooler still at 12–28°C, with the added pull of Diwali — Ram Ghat lit with thousands of diyas for Deepotsav, though accommodation needs booking weeks ahead if that's the draw.

## Planning around it

Chitrakoot is a short trip by design — 1 to 2 days is the ideal window on our data, reached via Allahabad or Khajuraho airports (130km) or the Chitrakoot Dham railhead. Infrastructure here is genuinely basic: dharamshala pilgrim lodging dominates, with budget days running near ₹500 and midrange closer to ₹1,850. [Full cost breakdown](/en/cost/chitrakoot).

## Verdict

- **October through March:** the parikrama works well across this whole window — pick October or November if Deepotsav timing matters to you.
- **April to June:** skip the barefoot circuit entirely; the heat risk is real, not seasonal caution for its own sake.
- **July to September:** possible, but monsoon flooding around the Mandakini adds real risk to the ghat sections — check conditions before committing.
