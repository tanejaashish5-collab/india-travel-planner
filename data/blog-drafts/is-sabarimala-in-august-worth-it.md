# Is Sabarimala in August worth it?

Short answer: our ledger scores [Sabarimala in August](/en/destination/sabarimala/august) at **2/10 — skip**. This isn't a weather-comfort call — the temple itself is closed for nearly all of the month.

## Why the temple is the whole story here

Sabarimala doesn't run on a typical open-season calendar. Our data notes the temple is closed except for a brief window on the first day of the Malayalam calendar month — a matter of hours, not a full-access period. August compounds that with monsoon: rains continuing through the month can make the pilgrimage routes into the hills impassable outright, on top of the temple already being shut.

It's worth clearing up a common assumption directly, because our data addresses it head-on: Onam, the major Kerala festival that often falls in August or early September, is **not Sabarimala-anchored**. There's no festival-driven reason to time a trip here around it. The ledger's verdict list for who should go this month is empty. Who should avoid it: everyone.

## When the temple is actually worth visiting

That "everyone avoid" verdict holds through September too — still closed except the same brief monthly window, monsoon trails still slippery, and September specifically carries peak pilgrim crowds tied to the run-up to the sacred season rather than genuine access. October doesn't improve matters either: it's pre-Mandala closure, with the temple opening only for that same one-day-a-month window until Mandala season itself opens **November 16–17**.

That date is the real answer to "when should I go instead." The Mandala–Makaravilakku pilgrimage season, running roughly mid-November through mid-January, is when Sabarimala actually functions as a destination — full access, the infrastructure built for it, and the reason the shrine draws the numbers it does.

## Planning around it

Sabarimala sits at 914m elevation in Kerala, reached via Cochin International Airport (170km) or Kottayam railway station (100km). The ideal stay, once the season is open, is short — 1 to 2 days. Costs stay simple year-round: budget pilgrimage stays run near ₹800 a day, midrange closer to ₹1,500. [Full cost breakdown here](/en/cost/sabarimala).

## Verdict

- **Any date between May and October:** skip, no exceptions — the temple access alone rules the trip out regardless of what else lines up.
- **Planning around Onam specifically:** skip — the festival has no Sabarimala tie-in; celebrate it elsewhere in Kerala instead.
- **Flexible dates:** wait for the Mandala season opening in mid-November, when the pilgrimage actually functions as one.
