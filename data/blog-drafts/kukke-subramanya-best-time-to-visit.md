# Kukke Subramanya weather by month — when to actually go

**Short answer:** November through February is a clean go — 10/10 in NakshIQ's dataset for three of those four months. June through August is a skip, scoring as low as 2/10. Everything else is a "wait" that depends on what you're there for.

## Why the swing is this wide

Kukke Subramanya sits in the Western Ghats at 120m elevation, and the destination's identity splits into two experiences that don't survive the monsoon equally. The temple itself — Sarpa Dosha rituals, daily darshan — runs year-round regardless of weather. The Kumara Parvatha trek to 1,712m does not: it's closed by the Forest Department for a stretch of the year, and that closure alone accounts for most of the swing in the month-by-month scores on the [destination page](/en/destination/kukke-subramanya/november).

## The peak window: November–February

These four months score 9–10/10. Rainfall drops under 5mm at the driest point (February), daytime temperatures sit in the 16–29°C range, and the Kumara Parvatha trek runs at what the dataset calls year-best dry-trail conditions. November specifically adds the six-day Champa Shashti festival, which pushes hotel rates up roughly 20% through the month — worth knowing if you're booking around it rather than despite it.

## The skip window: June–August

Three straight months score 1–2/10. The Western Ghats take the southwest monsoon at close to peak force here — rainfall runs 900–1,500mm across the three months, the Kumara Parvatha trek is closed outright, and the dataset notes town roads go on landslide-watch. Temple visits are still physically possible; the standard temple-plus-trek trip isn't. If your dates fall in this window, the honest move is to keep it to darshan only, not the full itinerary.

## The shoulder months

March holds up reasonably (8/10) before humidity and heat close the trek to dawn-only starts. April and May slide to "wait" territory as pre-monsoon heat builds — May specifically sees the trek close mid-month as the Forest Department prepares for the season. September is the mirror image on the other side: monsoon retreat, roads reopening, but the trek itself typically doesn't restart until October.

## What it costs

Peak-season (November–March) pricing: a mid-range hotel runs a typical ₹5,500 a night (range ₹3,200–9,400), a homestay around ₹3,200, food about ₹1,150 a day. Off-peak (May–August) rates drop by roughly half across the board. The temple itself also runs its own guest houses for pilgrims, a detail worth knowing if you're travelling on a tight budget. Full category breakdown on the [cost page](/en/cost/kukke-subramanya).

## The one-line answer

If the Kumara Parvatha trek is part of the plan, November through February is the only real window. If you're there for the temple alone, most months work — just not June through August.
