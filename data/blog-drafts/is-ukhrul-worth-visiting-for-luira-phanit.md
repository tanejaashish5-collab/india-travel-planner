# Is Ukhrul worth visiting for Luira Phanit?

Short answer: **wait, but go if the festival itself is the point.** Our ledger scores [Ukhrul in February](/en/destination/ukhrul/february) at **6/10 — wait**, not a strong outright month, but Luira Phanit is a genuine, centuries-old Tangkhul Naga festival with no substitute date.

## What Luira Phanit actually is

Luira Phanit — from "Lui" (field), "ra" (dig), and "phanit" (festival) — is the New Year and seed-sowing festival of the Tangkhul Naga tribe, marking the start of the agricultural season. It falls around February 15, tied to Lui-Ngai-Ni, the shared Naga calendar date, though individual villages may celebrate anywhere from January to March depending on local tradition. The festival includes indigenous games, music, dance, and folk performances, built around the Luithui Khami ritual: the village's Awunga (chief) and Awungva (chief queen) perform the first ceremonial seed-sowing, after which the rest of the village is permitted to sow its own fields.

## Why the month scores a "wait," not a "go"

Ukhrul sits at 1,662m in the Tangkhul Naga hills of Manipur, and our data is direct about February: the month works but isn't peak, sitting outside the destination's stronger March–May and October–December windows. What keeps it from being a write-off is that the Tangkhul villages don't shut down for winter — our data notes that villages stay genuinely active through February's cold, so a visitor sees real community life rather than a quiet, off-season shell of a destination.

## Who gets the most out of it

Our data flags two groups specifically: cultural trekkers, and weavers with an interest in Tangkhul textiles — both suited to a slower, culture-first visit rather than a checklist trip. On the other side, comfort-dependent travellers are flagged to avoid the month; Ukhrul in February is cold, remote, and light on tourist infrastructure.

## Getting there

Ukhrul is reached via Imphal, 83km away, or by rail via Dimapur, a considerably longer 260km haul. Neither is a quick transfer — factor a full travel day in either direction into the trip.

Ukhrul's cost data isn't broken out by exact month, but across the year a homestay runs roughly ₹1,450 in the quieter season up to ₹3,200 at peak, a mid-range hotel from ₹2,450 to ₹5,500, and food from ₹500 to ₹1,150 a day depending on season. ## Verdict

- **Luira Phanit is the reason for the trip:** go — February 15 (or the local village date) is the only window, and this is a rare, undertouristed cultural festival.
- **Flexible dates, festival isn't the draw:** wait — Ukhrul's stronger months (March–May, October–December) are a better standalone trip.
- **Comfort matters more than authenticity:** skip February specifically; the destination's data flags this month as unsuited to comfort-focused travel.
