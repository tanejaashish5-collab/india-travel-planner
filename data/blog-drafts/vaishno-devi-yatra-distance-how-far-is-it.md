# How far is the Vaishno Devi yatra?

Short answer: **13km**, on foot, from Katra to the Bhawan shrine — the figure our dataset carries from the Shrine Board's own altitude table, not a rounded estimate. Most yatris cover it in 1 to 3 days depending on pace, rest stops, and whether they continue on to Bhairavnath Temple afterward.

## The route, leg by leg

The trail runs through six official way-stations before the shrine, each with a Shrine Board altitude reading:

| Leg | Distance | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Katra → Banganga | 1km | Start of the yatra; Yatra Access Card counter at Katra bus stand. ~853m MSL. |
| Banganga → Charan Paduka | 1.5km (2.5km from Katra) | Sacred footprints site. ~1,030m MSL. |
| Charan Paduka → Adhkuwari | 3.5km (6km from Katra) | Major midpoint; ponies and palkis run up to here on the traditional route. ~1,463m MSL. |
| Adhkuwari → Himkoti | 2.5km (8.5km from Katra) | Rest house available. ~1,561m MSL. |
| Himkoti → Sanjichhat | 1km (9.5km from Katra) | Highest way-station; helipad location, Shrine Board langar. ~1,890m MSL. |
| Sanjichhat → Bhawan | 3.5km (13km from Katra) | Main shrine, the sacred cave of Mata Vaishno Devi. ~1,585m MSL. |

So **Charan Paduka to Adhkuwari is 3.5km**, and **Adhkuwari to Sanjichhat is 3.5km** (the Himkoti and Sanjichhat legs combined) — the two most-searched segment questions, both answered directly from the same official table.

Many pilgrims extend the trip by another 1.5km each way to Bhairavnath Temple past the Bhawan — tradition holds the yatra incomplete without it, though it isn't a requirement. That makes the full round including Bhairavnath roughly 14.5km one-way from Katra.

## How long it actually takes, and the alternatives

Our dataset lists the yatra as a **1 to 3 day** undertaking. A fit walker can do Katra to Bhawan and back in a single long day; spreading it to two or three days leaves room for rest at Adhkuwari or Sanjichhat and a visit to Bhairavnath. Ponies and palkis cover the traditional route as far as Adhkuwari; a helicopter service runs to Sanjichhat, leaving roughly 3.5km on foot either way; and a short ropeway connects Bhawan to Bhairavnath for anyone skipping that climb.

## When to actually walk it

The yatra runs year-round with no closed months, but conditions vary a lot. March through June and September through November are the dataset's strongest months — clear trails, full helicopter reliability, and (in October specifically) Sharad Navratri drawing 60-80K pilgrims a day, the single busiest stretch of the year. December holds up well too: cool, clear, with a year-end family pilgrimage rush around Christmas and New Year, though snowfall is possible on the Bhairon trail. July is the one genuine caution month — peak monsoon brings landslide-closure risk on the Adhkuwari-Sanjichhat-Bhawan stretch and a 30-40% jump in helicopter cancellations; the dataset marks it a "wait," not a go. January and February are workable but cold and quieter, with snow risk easing through February.

## What it costs

Katra's peak season (March-June, September-November) runs a mid-range hotel at a typical **₹6,300/night** (range ₹3,700-11,000), food at about ₹1,350 a day, and a full-day taxi at ₹5,000. The shoulder months (February, July, August, December) drop to roughly ₹4,400/night mid-range; January, Katra's quietest month, is cheaper still at around ₹2,850/night. ## Verdict

- **"How far is it":** 13km one-way on foot, 26km round trip, plus 3km more each way if you add Bhairavnath.
- **"How long does it take":** 1 day is doable; 2-3 days is more comfortable.
- **Best months:** March-June and September-November for reliability; December works but pack for cold; July is the month to avoid if you have a choice.
