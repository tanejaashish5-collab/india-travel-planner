# Happy when models improve (the positioning litmus)

**The test (a16z GP Anish Acharya, 2026-07):** *"Are you happy or sad when the models get better?"* Sad = your value is sophisticated engineering **around current model limits** — the next frontier release leapfrogs it, and the team carries a culture wound (the work they were proud of becomes deletable). Happy = you sit where model progress flows *through* you to the customer.

**The winning position, named:** be a **point of economic diffusion** for model progress into an industry segment — price, productize, package the intelligence primitive for buyers who can't consume raw models (his example: credit unions; ours: Indian-travel decisions, Canberra SMBs). The best spot is a *direct proxy*: model gets 2× better → your customer gets 2× more value.

## Applied to our assets

- **NakshIQ: passes cleanly.** Better models increase demand for verified grounding/citations and still can't fabricate India ground truth — the corpus appreciates with model progress. This is [[verification-as-moat]] restated from the capital side, and it's the same mechanic as [[aeo-citations-over-rankings]] (models need citable sources more, not less).
- **The Ganim/Canberra operator play: passes.** A local implementer's value = diffusing model progress into SMBs priced out of $50–100K/yr enterprise deployments ([[jason-lemkin]]'s numbers; the FDE hiring wave is the same signal at enterprise scale — see [[src-aishwarya-a16z-fde]]).
- **Content explainers/tutorials: fail.** Each model release obsoletes them (consistent with [[ai-content-saturation]]).

## Use

Ask it of every future ledger idea alongside the [[tata-gate]] and [[passion-fit-gate]] — a screening *question*, not (yet) a scored checklist factor. An idea that gets *worse* as models improve needs an extraordinary reason to proceed.
