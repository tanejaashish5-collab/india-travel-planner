# "No money down" is a security swap, not free money

**The pattern:** every structure that removes cash from a transaction works by **substituting a
security interest or a statutory liability for the cash**. The money doesn't disappear — the risk
moves onto the buyer's signature. So the question to ask of any "no money down" pitch is never *how
do I avoid paying?* but **"if I put in $0, what did I put in instead?"** — and the answer is always
findable in the security documents, never in the marketing.

Corollary, and the part that catches people: **the deals actually available at $0 down are
self-selecting for the worst assets.** A business that can clear a lender's bar attracts cash
buyers. One that can't is where full seller-financing shows up.

## The evidence (Australia, 2026-08-06)

**What replaces the cash**, verified against primary sources:

- **Lease** — landlord is entitled to demand a personal guarantee and routinely does for a buyer
  with no track record. NSW Small Business Commissioner: exposure runs to "savings, property or
  vehicles", and **"a company or trust structure may not protect a guarantor against personal
  liability"** — which defeats the standard "buy it through a company" answer.
- **Vendor finance** — the seller becomes the lender and takes a **General Security Agreement over
  the buyer's company _and assets_**.
- **Franchise/suppliers** — director guarantees, joint and several, surviving resignation.
- **Statutory, requiring no signature at all** — **Director Penalty Notices** for PAYG/GST/super
  (30-day window on inherited debt; **no grace period on anything accruing after you take over**;
  ATO issued 13,648 DPNs covering $1.88b in six months of 2023) and **insolvent trading s588G**
  (potentially unlimited compensation, personal bankruptcy, ASIC disqualification, up to 5 years'
  imprisonment where dishonesty is found).

**The structural irony:** a $0-down deal loads the business with debt service from day one, which
*raises* the insolvent-trading risk. The structure that removes the cash requirement increases the
odds of the liability that bankrupts you.

**And there is no $0 version anyway** — DD, legal and stamp duty run **5–15% of purchase price**
and cannot be financed. On a $250K business that's $12K–$37K in unavoidable cash.

## Why the geography matters more than the technique

The US playbook (Codie Sanchez et al.) rests on the **SBA 7(a) guaranteeing up to 90%** of a
purchase — canonical structure 80/10/10, only 10% buyer cash. **Australia has no equivalent**;
accountants are petitioning for one. Australian lenders want **20–40% down** and lend poorly against
goodwill. **A method can be entirely valid and still be non-transferable, because the enabling
institution doesn't exist in the new market.** Always check what government or lending
infrastructure a foreign playbook silently assumes.

## Base rates worth remembering

1-in-15 prospective buyers ever complete a purchase · 19–20 months search-to-close · ~100 businesses
reviewed per acquisition · 37% buy rate inside the best-regarded *paid* program (self-reported) ·
seller notes appear in **≤15%** of deals with sellers still taking **~84% cash at close** · **0 of
132** live Australian listings advertised vendor finance.

## The method note that generalises

One research leg traced a prominent, precise-looking Australian statistic ("42% of SME sales
incorporated vendor finance", attributed to CPA Australia) back to the cited survey and **found it
absent** — an unlinked "Key Statistics" box on an SEO legal-content page, with a companion figure
attributed to a body with no reason to track that data. **Precise unlinked numbers in stat boxes on
marketing pages should be treated as fabricated until verified at the institution itself.** Same
discipline as [[receipts-over-content]].

## Where it leaves us

The acquisition thesis is sound; the *vehicle* sold at seminars — a physical local business bought
with seller finance — carries lease, employee, tax and director exposure and is location-locked
(**119 of 132** Australian listings). Digital assets keep the thesis and drop nearly the whole
liability stack: no lease, no payroll, no stamp duty, location-independent, ~2× profit.

**And the sharpest version:** the best thing to buy is an asset with the defect you have already
personally fixed — which converts an acquisition from a bet on a method into
[[receipts-over-content]] applied to yourself.
