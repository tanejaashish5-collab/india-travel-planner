# src — Digital-asset acquisition screen (2026-08-08)

Founder approved spending remaining usage on the acquisition thread parked 08-07, after correcting
the geography premise ([[src-acquisition-viability-au]]): preferred shape is **digital · worldwide ·
Australia-based**. Screen brief: assets with **traffic but no monetisation** — NakshIQ's exact
defect — under US$50K, emphasis under US$20K.

**Verdict: DON'T BUY. Nothing worth buying at that price, the class is in the sharpest decline of
any tracked, and the one same-niche comp could not be given away.**

**This reverses my own 2026-08-06 recommendation.** I had pointed at digital assets as the category
surviving every constraint. Right about the constraints, wrong about the market — I hadn't yet
checked the inventory or the class. Recording the reversal rather than quietly restating.

## The number that ends it, in our exact niche

A **9-year-old India travel website**, listed on Flippa, asking **US$190**. **The auction expired
unsold.** That is the open market's price for a NakshIQ-shaped asset **with no owned audience
attached** — and it is the most useful figure in the screen.

## No quality inventory at our price

- **Empire Flippers** — cheapest content site across **34 sampled: $63,871. Zero under $50K.**
  Brokered M&A with 1–3 weeks staff vetting per deal; structurally can't serve sub-$50K.
- **Acquire.com** — **structurally excludes the profile**: *"we only list startups generating
  revenue"*; the pre-revenue exception is **SaaS-only, capped $25K**.
- **Flippa** — the only real sub-$20K supply, dominated by early-stage/templated/abandoned. The two
  genuine sub-$20K newsletters found were same-niche and **near-identically worded** (seller
  template, not organic supply).
- **SideProjectors** — clearest sub-$25K item: **$25,000 for 100 blogs**, one example earning **$83
  lifetime AdSense**.
- Real examples of the target profile exist at **$105K** (100K visitors/mo, $5.5K/mo revenue) — 5×
  above band, not inside it.

## The class is contracting — measured

- **Flippa H1 2026** (primary): content sales volume **−39% half-on-half, sharpest of any model
  tracked**; avg multiple **2.32x**; avg age at sale **10+ yrs (+29%)** — buyers now underwrite
  **survivorship**, not growth. YouTube outsold content for the first time.
- **Empire Flippers 2026 State of the Industry** (33pp): content = **13.3%** of deals; their own
  words — content sites *"have become inherently more risky than before and have an uncertain
  future"*; recommended buyer has deep technical SEO **or converts the site into owned
  products/services instead of ads-and-affiliate**. **Newsletters aren't even a tracked category.**
- **Pew** (non-interested, 68,879 tracked searches): AI summary present → link click **8%** vs
  **15%**; only **1%** click inside the summary.
- **Similarweb/Graphite**: aggregate top-40K only −2.5%, but losses concentrate in **ranks
  100–10,000** — giants absorb what mid-tier independents lose. **NakshIQ is in the damaged tier.**
- AI referral doesn't compensate: **+600K** against **−800M** monthly.
- **Recovery base rate:** ~400 algorithm-hit sites → only **22% recovered ≥20%** of lost traffic at
  11 months. **A cheap site with collapsed traffic is cheap for a terminal reason.**

## Flagged against our own interest

The *"first-hand verified content survived"* thesis — the one that would justify NakshIQ — is the
**weakest-evidenced claim in the screen**: small-N cases, proponents interested (a vendor selling
that exact fix; a vocal Google critic), and **Google's own docs say there is no AIO-specific content
requirement**. Do not price it as proven. Same discipline as [[receipts-over-content]].

## Verification is weak everywhere

**No marketplace treats third-party-audited financials as standard.** Every "verified" badge is
either a staffer eyeballing a screen-share or an API confirming **traffic only**. Empire Flippers has
the sole real audit trail — and **zero inventory in our band** to apply it to.

## The convergence

Three independent signals say one thing: **the traffic is the depreciating part; the owned audience
is the value.**

1. India travel site with traffic, no audience → **unsellable at $190**
2. Owned email lists **2.5–6.5x SDE** vs content **2.32x**, and structurally immune to the AIO
   mechanism (monetisation never routes through a Google click)
3. Empire Flippers' own prescription: **convert to owned products/services**

Which is exactly what [[map-not-territory-lead-magnet]] shipped one day before this screen ran. The
force destroying the thing I suggested buying is the force making the shortlist valuable.

**Sequence:** don't spend → the pre-registered 3-week conversion number is now the whole game →
revisit acquisition only from strength, and buy a **list or community**, never traffic.

**Money not spent is the deliverable.** The correct output of an evidence gate is often *don't*.

**Coverage caveat, stated not hidden:** Flippa's browse UI is JS-rendered and could not be swept
systematically, so its sub-$20K sample is opportunistic. More cheap inventory likely exists unseen —
but nothing class-level suggests it would be better.
