## The finding

**A 2.71M-subscriber channel with 0.53% reach, whose price ladder climbed to ₹5,90,000 as its
output fell 81%.** Detail on the operator: [[coach-bsr]].

## Three things it added to the wiki

1. **[[audience-written-social-proof]]** — the genuinely novel mechanic. He instructs viewers to
   comment their results **explicitly so other viewers read them and build belief**, stated on
   camera. 15/25 recent videos. Zero-cost, self-replenishing, read as peer evidence rather than
   marketing. Sansoni had to manufacture his testimonial wall; BSR's audience writes his.
2. **[[subscriber-count-is-a-past-receipt]]** — the convergence with
   [[src-digital-asset-screen]] **24 hours earlier**: a 44,000-subscriber newsletter earning
   $55/month, and a 2,710,000-subscriber channel getting 14,399 views. Same law at 1000× scale —
   **response is the asset; the audience number is a receipt for past work.**
3. **Third confirmation of the [[receipts-over-content]] inversion** — his two most expensive
   products are *Train The Trainer* (₹5,00,000) and *Coach to Highly Paid Coach*. After
   [[nitin-joshi]] (2026-08-03) and [[src-sansoni-funnel-teardown]] (2026-08-06), this is now a
   three-operator pattern, not an anecdote: **the top of a guru ladder sells the means to copy the
   guru.**

## The credential pattern repeats exactly

His *"first Indian to keynote the United Nations"* claim traces to Deccan Herald's
**`brandspot`/`pr-spot`** paid vertical (verified by direct fetch, HTTP 200), EverybodyWiki
(self-serve) and Passion Vista (paid feature). **This is the Forbes BRANDVOICE finding from the
Sansoni teardown, repeated on another continent.** Two for two.

**Generalised:** when a personal brand cites a masthead, check the URL path segment before the
article slug. `brandspot`, `pr-spot`, `brandvoice`, `partner-content` all mean *purchased*.
Stated precisely — **unverified, not disproven**; and no complaints or fraud reports exist.

## What it means for us

**Against our own interest, plainly:** this is not an opportunity, a gap, or a business to buy.
It is evidence that a 2.71M-sub Hindi self-improvement channel **decays to 0.5% reach and needs
₹5,00,000 "become a coach like me" packages to monetise**. That is a warning about the category
Chanakya sits beside, not an invitation into it.

Two usable outputs:
- **Chanakya:** a Devanagari affirmation-comment CTA is free, needs no new production, and fits the
  existing no-English-overlay rule. The one cheap testable borrow.
- **NakshIQ:** his device captures a **comment** (rented, non-portable); the August-shortlist offer
  captures an **email** (owned). His reach fell 81% with no recourse. That is
  [[owned-audience-funnel]] demonstrated at 2.71M scale rather than argued.

## Method error caught mid-scan

My first pitch-pattern scan ran **English** search terms against **Devanagari** transcripts and
returned near-nothing (3/25 on a single term) — which would have read as a clean finding. Re-run
with Devanagari terms: 16/25 "free", 15/25 comment-CTA, 11/25 "link below". Same class as the
`\b`-word-boundary failure already in memory: **an English regex over Hindi text fails silently and
looks like data.**
